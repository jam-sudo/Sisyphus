# Acoramidis parent-dose correction (2026-09-25)

The pinned Omega 2024 supplement records a fasted AG10-001 single-dose arm as 50 mg acoramidis with parent-plasma Cmax 2,110 ng/mL. Its aggregate was fitted against the free-acid SMILES with an unconverted 50 mg dose. The [PMDA review of the same study](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000278404.pdf) states that all doses in its clinical pharmacology section are **acoramidis hydrochloride** amounts (section 6, p. 23), then gives the AG10-001 50 mg/2,110 ng/mL arm (Table 23, p. 27). The [original phase 1 article](https://accp1.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cpdd.700) gives molecular weights of 292.13 for free acid and 328.77 for hydrochloride, and its Table 2 reports Cmax/D 47.5 ng/mL/mg and AUC0–24/D 387 ng·h/mL/mg for this arm. Those denominators match **50 × 292.13/328.77 = 44.4277 mg** parent: 2,110/44.4277 = 47.49 and 17,200/44.4277 = 387.15. This independent internal arithmetic supports retaining the measured parent Cmax and AUC while converting the administered dose.

The Cmax recipe now guards and converts the 50 mg aggregate to 44.4277 mg. All four Acoramidis dose arms in each exploratory MMPK table were converted with the same ratio. The archived PBPK-feature row was removed because its engine prediction used the old dose. Rebuilding CL/F and Vd/F from the corrected arms changed Acoramidis's derived CL/F from 0.8857 to 0.7870 mL/min/kg and Vd/F from 1.9420 to 1.7255 L/kg. The raw pinned Omega files remain unchanged. The fitted counts remain Cmax 906, CL/F 900, Vd/F 831.

After refitting, direct Cmax scaffold-CV AAFE is **3.3827** (previously 3.3699), with CL/F and Vd/F row-CV R² **0.220** and **0.353**. On the repeatedly accessed development N=73, direct ML AAFE is **2.9925** and Meta AAFE **2.8053** (conditional bootstrap 95% CI **2.2829–3.4882**); the paired Meta/ML ratio is **0.9374** (CI **0.8255–1.0625**). The nominal 90% development-residual interval has **9.91-fold** half-width and covers **66/73** consumed labels. These changes repair a dose-basis inconsistency; they are not independent accuracy evidence.
