# Additional parent-dose audit (2026-09-24)

The pinned Omega `approved.xlsx` records a salt for 269 names that remain in the fitted Cmax table. This is a **review queue**, not 269 errors: a source can state a parent-equivalent dose even when the formulation is a salt. For example, the [cysteamine primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2014194/) explicitly administered 1,200 mg **base** as 3,534 mg bitartrate; its fitted 1,200 mg dose stays unchanged. The original Omega CSV and workbooks remain unchanged. The Cmax recipe now checks each aggregate before conversion, and 14 matching rows in each exploratory MMPK table were dose-normalized. Four archived PBPK-feature rows were removed because their engine predictions used the old doses. Rebuilding `clf_training.csv` changed only the corresponding derived CL/F and Vd/F values; its 1,007 names and fitted counts (901 and 832) remain unchanged. The static console's metformin preset now uses 389.93 mg parent for a 500 mg HCl tablet, and its dose input identifies the parent-active-moiety basis.

The selected metformin aggregate combines three 500 mg studies. The 2012 study identifies HCl in its abstract; the two older numeric arms were not independently re-read, so their dose-basis judgment also relies on the pinned workbook's `hydrochloride` annotation and the product-strength convention. Trospium's selected numeric peak comes from a review, not an independently checked original table.

After refitting and regenerating the **consumed** N=73 development benchmark, direct ML AAFE is **3.0398** and Meta AAFE **2.8311** (conditional bootstrap 95% CI **2.3057–3.5251**). The paired Meta/ML ratio is **0.9313** (CI **0.8201–1.0541**). Direct Cmax scaffold-CV AAFE is **3.3439**. The nominal 90% development-residual half-width is **9.84-fold**, covering **66/73** repeatedly used development labels. These shifts do not establish external improvement. The remaining salt queue requires source-specific adjudication; mass-converting every salt-tagged row would introduce errors.
