# Benazepril hydrochloride dose-basis correction (2026-09-25)

The pinned Omega `approved.xlsx` workbook (SHA256 `4521a2d89dde71c9e9381ab0a920e84dfc69c714860de377cd73b338572026d2`, row 512) records a 10 mg **hydrochloride** capsule, parent benazepril SMILES (CAS 86541-75-5), and parent Cmax **135.41 ng/mL**. It cites [Kaiser et al. 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2758102/) (PMID 2758102; DOI 10.1002/bdd.2510100404). The original abstract calls the administered compound benazepril.HCl, reports a single oral 10 mg dose in 62 healthy men, and distinguishes plasma benazepril from benazeprilat. A [DailyMed benazepril hydrochloride label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06f1a636-2306-4bbb-9573-cf461adda04c) explicitly defines 10 mg tablet strength as hydrochloride mass and gives its molecular weight as **460.96 g/mol**. [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Benazepril) gives parent benazepril's molecular weight as about **424.5 g/mol**; the workbook's structure-derived value is **424.497 g/mol**.

The fitted structure and inference contract use parent benazepril, so the 10 mg hydrochloride dose converts to **9.2089769 mg parent** (`10 × 424.497 / 460.96`). The Cmax and AUC remain as recorded in Omega; this is a dose-basis correction, not a concentration reassignment. The original full numeric table and capsule batch record were not accessible, so the 135.41 ng/mL figure remains workbook-sourced and the original capsule's exact labeling convention is inferred from the paper's hydrochloride wording plus the regulatory salt-strength convention.

The guarded Cmax recipe and both exploratory MMPK tables now use the parent-equivalent dose; the stale PBPK-feature row for the old 10 mg input was removed.
