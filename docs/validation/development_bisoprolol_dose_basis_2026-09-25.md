# Bisoprolol hemifumarate dose-basis correction (2026-09-25)

The pinned Omega `approved.xlsx` workbook (SHA256 `4521a2d89dde71c9e9381ab0a920e84dfc69c714860de377cd73b338572026d2`, rows 562 and 564–567) records bisoprolol **fumarate** tablets at 5, 10, 20, and 40 mg, while its SMILES and molecular weight (325.449 g/mol) describe parent bisoprolol. The [1994 original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7962671/) explicitly calls its 5–40 mg single oral doses **bisoprolol hemifumarate** in eight healthy men. The second [10 mg bioequivalence study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23006443/) describes a fasted bisoprolol tablet arm but its accessible abstract does not state the salt; the workbook marks it as fumarate. The [FDA Zebeta label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2007/019982s014lbl.pdf) and [USP monograph](https://doi.usp.org/USPNF/USPNF_M9792_04_01.html) identify marketed bisoprolol fumarate tablet strengths and the 2:1 parent:fumarate stoichiometry. The full numeric tables and the second study's batch composition were unavailable, so its 41.21 ng/mL Cmax and specific formulation basis remain workbook-sourced.

USP gives the salt formula mass as **766.96 g/mol for two bisoprolol molecules**. Parent-equivalent dose is therefore reported fumarate mass × `2 × 325.449 / 766.96` = **0.84867268** × reported mass. The 10 mg fitted Cmax aggregate is now **8.48673 mg parent**; its 36.314 ng/mL geometric aggregate Cmax remains unchanged.
